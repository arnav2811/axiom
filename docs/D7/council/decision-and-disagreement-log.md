## Decisions

### Decision D7-D1: Evaluation Method
**Date:** D7 Preparation
**Participants:** Arnav, Keshav
**Decision:** Think-aloud usability testing (concurrent, not retrospective)
**Rationale:** User-centred (rubric requirement); rich qualitative data; feasible with 5-6 participants; lab-compatible
**Alternatives Considered:** Cognitive walkthrough (rejected — expert-based, not user-centred); questionnaire-only (rejected — misses cognitive processes); heuristic evaluation (rejected — explicitly disallowed as sole method)
**Status:** Accepted ✅

### Decision D7-D2: Number of Participants
**Date:** D7 Preparation
**Participants:** Arnav, Keshav
**Decision:** Target N=5-6 participants
**Rationale:** Nielsen (2000): 5 participants find ~85% of usability problems; 6 provides buffer for no-shows
**Alternatives Considered:** N=3 (rejected — too few for saturation); N=10 (rejected — impractical for lab sessions with time constraints)
**Status:** Accepted ✅

## Disagreements

### Disagreement D7-DIS-1: Think-Aloud Variant

**Topic:** Whether to use concurrent or retrospective think-aloud
**Arnav's Position:** Concurrent — captures real-time cognitive processes; more natural
**Keshav's Position:** Retrospective — doesn't interfere with task performance; may be more accurate
**Discussion:** Arnav referenced Ericsson & Simon (1993) showing concurrent think-aloud has minimal interference for routine tasks. Keshav agreed that our tasks are straightforward enough that concurrent verbalisation won't significantly impact performance.
**Resolution:** Concurrent think-aloud selected. Both satisfied.
**Impact:** None — resolved during D7 preparation.
