# Facilitator Script — Usability Evaluation Session

**Study:** Footprint Lens Usability Evaluation  
**Method:** Moderated Think-Aloud Usability Testing  
**Duration:** ~25–30 minutes per participant

---

## Phase 1: Welcome & Consent (5 minutes)

### Welcome Script

> *"Hello, and thank you for taking the time to help us today. My name is [Moderator Name], and this is [Observer Name] who will be taking notes.*
>
> *We're working on a desktop application called Footprint Lens for our COMP2300 group project, and we'd like your help to evaluate how easy it is to use. I want to emphasise — we are testing the software, not you. There are no right or wrong answers, and you cannot do anything wrong during this session.*
>
> *The session will take about 25 to 30 minutes. I'll ask you to complete 5 short tasks using some pre-printed scenario cards. The cards contain fictional data — we won't ask you to enter any of your own personal information.*
>
> *You can stop at any time, skip any task, or ask questions. Your participation is completely voluntary.*
>
> *Before we begin, I'd like to go through the information sheet and consent form with you."*

### Actions
1. Hand the participant the **Participant Information Sheet**.
2. Allow them to read it. Answer any questions.
3. Hand them the **Consent Form**.
4. Participant signs the consent form. Assign participant code (P01, P02, etc.).
5. Store consent form separately from observation sheets.

---

## Phase 2: Think-Aloud Introduction (2 minutes)

> *"During the tasks, I'd like you to think aloud — that means telling us what you're looking at, what you're trying to do, what you expect to happen, and anything that confuses or surprises you.*
>
> *For example, if I were trying to find a file on this computer, I might say: 'I'm looking at the desktop... I expect to see a Documents folder... I'm going to click on it... I thought it would open a file browser but it opened something else, that's confusing.'*
>
> *If you go quiet for a while, I might gently remind you by saying 'What are you thinking right now?' — that's not because you're doing anything wrong, it's just to keep the think-aloud going.*
>
> *Do you have any questions before we start?"*

---

## Phase 3: Task Execution (15–18 minutes)

### General Instructions

- Present **one task card at a time**, in order (T1 through T5).
- Ask the participant to **read the card aloud** before starting.
- **Do not coach** the participant. Only provide assistance if they are completely stuck for more than 60 seconds.
- If they ask "Am I doing this right?", respond neutrally: *"There's no right or wrong way. Just do what feels natural."*
- Use think-aloud prompts if silent for >15 seconds: *"What are you thinking right now?"* or *"What are you looking at?"*
- After each task, administer the **Single Ease Question (SEQ)**: *"On a scale of 1 to 7, where 1 is very difficult and 7 is very easy, how easy or difficult was this task?"*

### Task Cards (hand one at a time)

**Card T1:**
> "You live in a shared 3-bedroom student apartment in Bengaluru. Your electricity sub-meter reads 120 kWh for October. Record this entry as household electricity and check what carbon estimate is calculated."

**Card T2:**
> "You are sceptical of the carbon estimate just displayed. Find out which emission factor was used, where it comes from, and whether it is verified or illustrative."

**Card T3:**
> "You work a part-time night shift and must travel 12 km home by taxi at 2:30 AM for personal safety. Record this journey as a protected choice. Check what action recommendations are offered."

**Card T4:**
> "Your kitchen mixer-grinder broke down. Instead of discarding it, you paid a technician to repair it. Record this repair activity and explain what the system says about emissions."

**Card T5:**
> "Navigate to the summary view. What is the total household electricity footprint? Does the system divide this between roommates or keep it as a shared total? Explain why."

### Between Tasks
- Reset application state if required (Keshav operates system reset).
- Record task completion, errors, help requests, and comprehension answer on observation sheet.

---

## Phase 4: Post-Task Questionnaire & Debrief (5 minutes)

> *"Thank you for completing the tasks. Now I'd like you to fill in a brief questionnaire about your overall experience with the system. It has 10 statements — just rate each one from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). Don't overthink it — your first reaction is usually the best."*

1. Hand the participant the **SUS Questionnaire** (see post-task-questionnaire.md).
2. Wait for them to complete it.

### Debrief Questions

> *"Before we finish, I have a few quick questions:"*

1. *"How transparent did you find the calculation formula and sources?"*
2. *"Did you find the protected choice concept respectful and useful?"*
3. *"How did you feel about non-numeric categories remaining descriptive rather than showing numbers?"*
4. *"What was the most confusing or frustrating aspect of the interface?"*
5. *"Is there anything you expected the system to do that it didn't?"*

Record responses on the observation sheet.

---

## Phase 5: Close (2 minutes)

> *"Thank you so much for your time today — your feedback is really valuable for improving our system. If you have any questions about the study or want to know the results, please feel free to contact us. Have a great day!"*

---

## Moderator Reminders

- ✅ Stay neutral — do not react positively or negatively to participant actions
- ✅ Do not answer questions about the system during tasks — redirect: *"What would you try?"*
- ✅ Record participant code on all materials
- ✅ Ensure consent form is signed before starting tasks
- ✅ Reset the application between participants (clear local CSV data)
- ❌ Do not ask about personal consumption, bills, or lifestyle
- ❌ Do not coach, hint, or guide during tasks
