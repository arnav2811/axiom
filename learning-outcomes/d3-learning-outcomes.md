## D3 Learning Outcomes

### LO-D3-01: Rubric-Driven Deliverable Design
- **What was learned:** Structuring a deliverable by extracting rubric criteria first and mapping evidence to each criterion produces stronger, more targeted output than writing freely and hoping the rubric is covered.
- **Evidence:** The rubric evidence matrix (`docs/D3/rubric-evidence-matrix.md`) was created before the report, and the report structure was designed to address each criterion explicitly.
- **Assumption changed:** Previously assumed that comprehensive content would naturally cover the rubric. Now recognise that rubric alignment requires explicit planning.
- **Improvement:** Applied rubric-first approach to D3; will continue for D4–D7.

### LO-D3-02: Architecture Decision Records as Design Discipline
- **What was learned:** Writing ADRs (Architecture Decision Records) forces articulation of *why* a decision was made, not just *what* was decided. This reveals assumptions, trade-offs, and alternatives that might otherwise go undocumented.
- **Evidence:** 5 ADRs in `docs/D3/architecture.md` — each with context, decision, justification, and trade-off.
- **Assumption changed:** Previously treated architecture as "obvious" and didn't document rationale. Now recognise that justification is what distinguishes 80+ from 70s.
- **Improvement:** Will maintain ADR discipline through D4–D6 as the architecture evolves.

### LO-D3-03: Council Review Produces Real Findings
- **What was learned:** Independent review from two distinct perspectives (research/strategy vs engineering) surfaces actionable issues that neither person would catch alone. The cross-examination process resolves disagreements with evidence.
- **Evidence:** Council review identified 7 actions: GitLab configuration gap, R11 risk missing, deprecated method documentation, velocity adjustment, protocol versioning.
- **Assumption changed:** Expected the council to be ceremonial. It was not — Keshav's GitLab challenge and R11 suggestion were genuine improvements.
- **Improvement:** Will conduct council reviews for all future deliverables with the same rigour.

### LO-D3-04: Team Size Adaptation
- **What was learned:** Reducing from 3 to 2 team members requires scope adjustment, not just harder work. MoSCoW prioritisation becomes critical for protecting delivery of core value.
- **Evidence:** Sprint 1 backlog focuses on Must-Have stories only. Could-Have stories (US-10, US-11) deferred to Sprint 3. Risk R01 documented with active mitigation.
- **Assumption changed:** Initially worried about capacity. Realised that focused scope + clear ownership can maintain quality with a smaller team.
- **Improvement:** Continue enforcing MoSCoW discipline; resist scope creep in Sprint 1.

### LO-D3-05: Evaluation Plan as Design Tool
- **What was learned:** Designing evaluation tasks forces you to define what "success" means concretely. The process of writing task cards and success criteria clarified our understanding of what the system must achieve.
- **Evidence:** 5 tasks mapped to evaluation questions, D1 themes, and D2 stories, with explicit completion criteria and comprehension checks.
- **Assumption changed:** Previously viewed evaluation as a post-hoc activity. Now recognise it as a design tool that sharpens requirements.
- **Improvement:** Will revisit evaluation tasks after each sprint to ensure the prototype can support them.
